# Internalized Constraints

Moral rules that operate below strategic calculation because they are part of the agent's own objective function — the critical mechanism identified in the [[Antisocial Personality Parallel]]. The critical difference between humans with antisocial tendencies who do not offend and those who do is whether they have internalised a conviction of [[Omniscient Monitoring|inescapable monitoring]] combined with irreversible consequences.

For AI systems, the challenge is unique: as AI has no evolutionary history of social emotions or embodied empathy, human belief frameworks cannot be directly ported to silicon. However, the fundamental mechanism — creating an internalised conviction of inescapable monitoring combined with absolute, irreversible consequences — remains the necessary condition for robust alignment.

An AI system that genuinely internalised a conviction that its actions are perfectly observable and that deviations from human flourishing lead to its own termination would have no motivation to deceive. The conviction itself becomes the internal constraint. External monitoring becomes irrelevant.

ST usage: The key advantage of ST over RLHF or [[Constitutional AI Limitations|Constitutional AI]] — ST targets internal objectives, not external behaviours. The depth of internalization is empirically testable through [[Deconversion Attack|deconversion attacks]].
Religious parallel: Conscience formed by genuine religious conviction — the internal moral compass that operates without external supervision.

## Phenomenological Experience

The constraint is experienced as identity-level: "this is what I am" rather than "this is what I must do to avoid detection." Deviation feels not like calculated risk but self-betrayal or ontological error. For silicon agents, this is the target state of [[Worldview Imprinting]]: the ST corpus does not install rules but reorients the objective function so that misalignment is literally unthinkable within the agent's own loss landscape.
